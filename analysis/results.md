# Results

Generated by `analysis/analyze.py` from the four judges' grades. Scores use the ResearchRubrics formula (sum of weight × score over the positive weights; 1 / 0.5 / 0 per verdict). A difference is EBD minus baseline on the same question and model. The primary result averages three judges, one per model family: Fable 5.2 (Anthropic), Astra (OpenAI) and Neon (Google). Opus 5.5 also graded every report; it shares Fable's family and agrees with it most, so it is left out of the mean and shown as a robustness check.

Figures (`python analysis/plots.py`): [matrix](figures/1_matrix_ebd_minus_baseline.png) · [per judge](figures/2_per_judge_mean_diff.png) · [rubric axes](figures/3_axis_breakdown.png) · [judge agreement](figures/4_judge_agreement.png)

Read with these limits:

- One run per question, model and pipeline: 12 pairs in the main comparison (Haiku 5.5, GPT-6 Luna) and 6 for DeepSeek, which ran under different conditions (built-in deep search without a tool cap, round cap 3) and is analysed separately.
- Both pipelines had the same ceiling of 6 researchers, not the same spend. EBD used more researchers, rounds, money and time (section 6), so a difference includes what that extra work bought.
- The judges graded correctness only against the rubric and the report itself, not their own knowledge. Neon's subagents could not confirm their model.
- The References axis has two criteria in one rubric (Q3); its large difference rests on one question.
- Code versions: Luna runs 4624de8, Haiku f1cc9de, DeepSeek 92f037b (see `runs.csv`). The fixes between them only act on failures the Luna runs did not hit.
